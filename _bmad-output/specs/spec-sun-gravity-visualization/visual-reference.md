## Confirmed design

- Separate schematic scene, opened by Sun’s Gravity with a Back action.
- A dominant solar well plus smaller depressions traveling with each of the eight planets.
- Compressed orbital spacing with calculated orbital timing, rather than illustrative speeds.
- Bodies appear along the schematic surface. Its height does not represent actual planetary ecliptic height.

## Draft visual assumptions

- Open at a still oblique angle showing depth and all eight planets. Manual orbit and zoom reveal the surface; Reset View restores the composition.
- Use a bounded smooth central depression that flattens outward. Smaller local dips blend into the sheet without seams, spikes or flicker as bodies move.
- Keep the Sun’s well dominant and planetary dips readable. Larger planetary masses should produce stronger illustrative dips where practical; exaggeration prevents quantitative comparison.
- Enhance body sizes independently of scientific-view scaling for readability; disclose this. Preserve recognizable body appearance and legible labels, including Saturn’s rings.
- Keep wire density and brightness restrained, fade boundaries, and prevent the sheet from obscuring bodies or selection. A higher camera angle may flatten the apparent well; Reset View must recover its depth.
- Use no decorative grid waves, automatic orbiting camera, speed-reactive glow or inward-spiral motion. Well movement follows planetary positions during playback and scrubbing.

## Explanation and scientific grounding

Visible disclosure: **Schematic — distances compressed; well depths and body sizes exaggerated.**

Explain this view should communicate that the Sun’s gravity curves planetary motion while planets continue moving sideways; the sheet is an explanatory analogy. Real planets do not roll on a membrane or lose orbital energy through sheet friction. The grid does not calculate spacetime curvature; timing and physical information come from the existing JPL approximate orbital model.
